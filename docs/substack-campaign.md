# Substack republishing campaign

Moving the 14 archive essays from thetocque.org onto the Substack **one at a
time**, each with a social push on LinkedIn, X, and Facebook.

## The tradeoff, and how we handle it

Substack cannot set a canonical URL pointing at an external site. So every
republished essay exists twice, and Google picks a winner — usually the higher
authority domain, which is Substack. The version that ranks would then be the
one *without* the donate button.

Three things keep that acceptable:

1. **Spacing.** One a week, not 14 at once. Google sees a trickle, and the
   thetocque.org originals stay first-indexed and older, which usually wins.
2. **Attribution link.** Every Substack post ends with a link back to the
   original. Standard republishing convention, and it passes a signal home.
3. **thetocque.org keeps its canonical self-referential.** We are not ceding
   the archive — the site stays the system of record.

If a specific essay starts ranking on Substack above the site and that matters,
the fix is to shorten the Substack version to an excerpt plus a "read the rest"
link. Don't pre-emptively cripple all 14.

## Sequencing note: publish before you import

Substack emails subscribers on every publish. Import the WordPress subscriber
list **after** drops 1–3 are live, so:

- nobody gets three old essays in their inbox in one week, and
- arrivals from the import find a publication with content rather than an empty
  page.

From drop 4 onward, open each post with a one-line "From the archive —
originally published April 2026" so it never reads as passing off old work as
new.

# Drop 1 — Liberty's Three-Legged Stool

**Original:** https://thetocque.org/2026/04/29/libertys-three-legged-stool/
**Image:** the labelled stool illustration (ECONOMICS / FAITH / CIVICS)

### Substack subtitle

> Tocqueville said freedom rests on three supports. Two centuries later, all
> three are wobbling.

### Social — LinkedIn

> A stool with three legs is a clever piece of engineering. Strong and equal,
> it holds. Weaken one leg and the whole thing goes over.
>
> That was Alexis de Tocqueville's picture of American liberty in 1831. He
> argued freedom rests on three supports, and that none of them survives alone:
>
> — Faith: the moral foundation that teaches duty over desire.
> — Economics: sound money that rewards work, saving, and honest trade.
> — Civics: the habits of self-government, citizens rather than spectators.
>
> His warning was blunt. The three must stay in balance. America's genius was
> never only the Constitution — it was the character that made self-government
> possible in the first place.
>
> Look at the stool today. The faith leg has weakened. The money leg has been
> debased. A great many citizens feel no connection at all to the civic leg.
>
> That is not three separate problems. It is one stool, and it is getting
> shaky.
>
> I've started writing about what it would take to strengthen all three. First
> essay linked below.
